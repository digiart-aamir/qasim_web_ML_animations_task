Worked example: how this prediction is calculated

1. Measure distance

For each training example, subtract its area from the query area and take the absolute value.

2. Choose the nearest K

Sort the distances from smallest to largest. Keep only the first K examples and their prices.

3. Average their prices

Y is measured in thousands of dollars here. Add the selected Y values and divide by K to predict the price.

Learning notes

Understand the idea behind the graph

Short explanations of the concepts represented in the computation graph.

01 / LEARNING TYPE

Supervised learning

KNN learns from labelled examples. Each training observation has an input feature (X) and a known target (Y). In this example, X is house area and Y is house price.

Training example = (x, y)
02 / TASK

Regression

Regression predicts a continuous numerical value, such as a house price, salary or temperature. KNN regression averages the target values of nearby examples.

Input: 1,600 sq ft → Price: ?
03 / DISTANCE

Measure closeness

For this one-feature demonstration, distance is the absolute difference between a training observation and the query value.

d(x, xq) = |x − xq|
04 / NEIGHBORS

What does K mean?

K is the number of nearby training examples used for prediction. KNN calculates distances, sorts them in ascending order and selects the first K observations.

Select the K smallest distances
05 / PREDICTION

Calculate the mean

After selecting neighbors, KNN adds their target values and divides by K. This arithmetic mean is the final prediction.

ŷ = (y₁ + y₂ + ... + yₖ) / K
06 / MODEL BEHAVIOR

Small K vs. large K

Small K is more sensitive to local examples and noise. Large K produces smoother predictions but can miss local patterns. The best K depends on the dataset.

Small K: local · Large K: smoother
