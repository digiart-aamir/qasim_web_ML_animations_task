Theoretical Comparison

Linear vs. Polynomial Regression: When to Use What

Dimension Linear Regression ($y = mx + b$) Polynomial Regression ($y = \sum a_i x^i$)
Hypothesis Shape Strictly straight line (1st order degree) Curved line with up to $n-1$ local extrema / inflection points
Degrees of Freedom 2 parameters: slope ($m$) and intercept ($b$) $d + 1$ parameters (one for each power $x^0, x^1, \dots, x^d$)
Bias / Variance High Bias, Low Variance: Will not overfit, but may underfit non-linear data Low Bias, High Variance: Can fit complex curves, but high risk of overfitting with high degrees
Sensitivity to Outliers Outliers shift slope & intercept proportionally High-degree polynomials oscillate wildly near boundaries (Runge's phenomenon)
Extrapolation Safety Predicts linear trend beyond range with expanding confidence interval Extremely hazardous beyond observed $X$ boundaries ($x^n$ diverges rapidly)
Interpretability Very High: $m$ is the direct unit change in $Y$ per unit of $X$ Moderate to Low: Individual higher-degree coefficients lack intuitive single-unit meanings
