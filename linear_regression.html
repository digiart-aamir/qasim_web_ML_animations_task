Mathematical Theory & Foundations

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) is the fundamental bedrock of regression analysis and statistical learning. Below are the exact mathematical derivations, geometric interpretations, and live calculations using your active dataset.

Geometry & Variance

Why "Least Squares"? ($R^2$ Decomposition)

OLS squares each residual error $e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$. Squaring penalizes larger mistakes quadratically and cancels out negative signs. Total variance in $Y$ is cleanly partitioned into explained and unexplained variation:

Total Sum of Squares (SS_tot):
SS_tot = Σ(y - ȳ)² = —
(Total variance of Y around its baseline mean)
Residual Sum of Squares (SS_res):
SS_res = Σ(y - ŷ)² = —
(Remaining variance unexplained by the line)
R-Squared (Coefficient of Determination):
R² = 1 - (SS_res / SS_tot) = —
(Proportion of variance explained by the model)
Geometric Theorem

The Centroid Invariant $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$

A fundamental property of Ordinary Least Squares is that the optimal fitted line always passes directly through the mean point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. This is because:

b = ȳ - m·x̄ ==> ȳ = m·x̄ + b

When you toggle the Centroid layer in the lab above, notice the crosshairs marker. Even as you drag or add points, the regression line continuously pivots around this balance point.

Statistical Assumptions

Gauss-Markov Theorem & BLUE

The Gauss-Markov theorem states that under five standard conditions, the OLS estimator is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE) with minimum variance:

  • Linearity in Parameters: The relationship between $X$ and $Y$ is linear.
  • Exogeneity: Expected value of residuals given $X$ is zero: $E[\epsilon | X] = 0$.
  • Homoscedasticity: Constant residual variance across all values of $X$.
  • No Autocorrelation: Residuals of different observations are uncorrelated.
  • Non-Collinearity: Sufficient variance in $X$ ($SS_{xx} > 0$).
Machine Learning

Analytical OLS vs. Gradient Descent

Why use Gradient Descent when an exact analytical formula exists? For a single variable, closed-form $O(1)$ computation is fastest. However, in machine learning:

Closed-Form (Normal Eq): $(X^T X)^{-1} X^T y$
Matrix inversion takes $O(d^3)$ time, becoming intractable when features $d > 10,000$.

Gradient Descent: Iterative updates $w \leftarrow w - \alpha \nabla J(w)$ scaling efficiently to millions of features and online streaming batches.
Diagnostics

Outliers, Leverage, & Influence

Not all outliers have equal influence on the slope:

Leverage measures how far an observation's $X$ value is from the mean $\bar{x}$. An outlier near $\bar{x}$ only shifts the intercept, but an outlier far along the $X$-axis exerts immense leverage like a lever, tilting the entire slope drastically (try the "High Leverage Outlier" preset above to observe this effect!).
